## 1. Section 5-12-070: Landlord's Responsibility to Maintain

**Source:** Chicago Municipal Code, Chapter 5-12, Section 5-12-070
**Official Code URL:** https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/chicago/latest/chicago_il/0-0-0-2639121

### Statutory Language (Core Obligation)

> "The landlord shall maintain the premises in compliance with all applicable provisions of the municipal code and shall promptly make any and all repairs necessary to fulfill this obligation."

### Key Points

- The landlord's duty to maintain applies to the **entire property** -- both the individual dwelling unit AND common areas shared with other tenants.
- This includes **elevators**, stairwells, hallways, porches, windows, floors, walls, and ceilings.
- The duty encompasses compliance with **all applicable provisions of the municipal code**, which includes the Chicago Building Code, the Conveyance Device Code (Chapter 14X-10), and all safety standards.
- Failure to maintain elevators in compliance with the municipal code constitutes **material noncompliance** with Section 5-12-070, triggering tenant remedies under Section 5-12-110.

### Scope of Obligation

The landlord must:
- Maintain the structural integrity of the building
- Keep floors, walls, stairwells, porches, windows, elevators, and ceilings in good repair
- Comply with all applicable building code provisions
- Promptly make all necessary repairs
- Maintain common areas in a fit and habitable condition

## 2. Section 5-12-110: Tenant Remedies

**Source:** Chicago Municipal Code, Chapter 5-12, Section 5-12-110
**Official Code URL:** https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/chicago/latest/chicago_il/0-0-0-2639177

Section 5-12-110 is the primary remedial section of the RLTO. It provides tenants with multiple remedies when the landlord materially breaches maintenance obligations under Section 5-12-070. The section contains subsections (a) through (f), each addressing different remedy scenarios.

### Subsection (a): Noncompliance by Landlord -- Lease Termination

If there is material noncompliance by the landlord with a rental agreement or with Section 5-12-070 **that renders the premises not reasonably fit and habitable**, the tenant may:

- Deliver a **written notice** to the landlord specifying the acts and/or omissions constituting the material noncompliance
- Specify that the rental agreement will **terminate on a date not less than 14 days** after receipt of the notice by the landlord
- Unless the material noncompliance is **remedied within the specified time period**
- If terminated, the landlord must **return all prepaid rent and security deposits**

### Subsection (b): Failure to Deliver Possession

If the landlord fails to deliver possession of the dwelling unit in compliance with the rental agreement or Section 5-12-070:
- Rent shall **abate** until possession is delivered
- The tenant may **terminate the rental agreement** upon written notice and receive all prepaid rent and security
- OR the tenant may **demand performance** and maintain an action for possession and recover damages

### Subsection (c): Repair and Deduct (Minor Defects)

For defects costing **$500 or less** (or half the monthly rent, whichever is greater, but not to exceed one month's rent):
- Tenant must **notify the landlord in writing**
- **Wait 14 days** for the landlord to make repairs
- If the landlord fails to repair, the tenant may **make the repairs themselves**
- Upon presentation of **paid receipts** to the landlord, the tenant may **deduct the cost from rent**

### Subsection (d): Rent Withholding (Failure to Maintain)

> "If there is material noncompliance by the landlord with the rental agreement or with Section 5-12-070, the tenant may notify the landlord in writing of the tenant's intention to withhold from the monthly rent an amount which reasonably reflects the reduced value of the premises due to the material noncompliance. If the landlord fails to correct the condition within 14 days after being notified by the tenant in writing, the tenant may, during the time such failure continues, deduct from the rent the stated amount."

**Key requirements:**
1. Written notice from the tenant to the landlord
2. Statement of intent to withhold an amount reflecting the "reduced value" of the premises
3. 14-day period for the landlord to correct the condition
4. If landlord fails to correct within 14 days, tenant may deduct/withhold the stated amount

**Important restriction:** A tenant shall NOT withhold rent under this subsection if the condition was caused by the deliberate or negligent act or omission of the tenant, a member of the tenant's family, or other person on the premises with the tenant's consent.

### Subsection (e): Damages and Injunctive Relief

If there is material noncompliance by the landlord with the rental agreement or with Section 5-12-070:
- The tenant may **obtain injunctive relief** and/or **recover damages** by claim or defense
- This subsection does **not preclude** the tenant from obtaining other relief under the RLTO chapter

### Subsection (f): Failure to Provide Essential Services

If there is material noncompliance constituting an **immediate danger to health and safety**, or if the landlord fails to supply **heat, running water, hot water, electricity, gas, or plumbing**, the tenant may give written notice and then exercise the following remedies:

1. **Procure substitute services** and deduct costs from rent upon presentation of paid receipts
2. **Recover damages** based on the reduction in the fair rental value of the dwelling unit
3. **Procure substitute housing** -- tenant is excused from paying rent for the period of noncompliance; tenant may recover the cost of substitute housing up to an amount equal to the monthly rent (prorated)
4. **Withhold rent** after **24-hour notice** -- if landlord fails to correct within 24 hours, tenant may withhold rent reflecting reduced value
5. **Terminate the lease** after **72-hour notice** -- if the material noncompliance or failure persists for more than 72 hours after tenant notification, the tenant may terminate by written notice

**Exception:** No termination shall be allowed if the failure is due to the inability of the utility provider to provide service.

### Definition of Material Noncompliance (Elevator Provision)

For purposes of Section 5-12-110, **material noncompliance with Section 5-12-070 includes, but is not limited to**, the enumerated list of failures (see Section 8 below). Critically, this list includes:

> "Failure to maintain elevators in compliance with applicable provisions of the municipal code"

## 3. Tenant Remedy Process: Step-by-Step

### For General Material Noncompliance (e.g., Elevator Failure)

**Step 1: Document the Problem**
- Photograph/video the broken elevator, out-of-service signs, etc.
- Note dates and times when the elevator is nonfunctional
- Save any communications with landlord/management about the issue
- File a 311 complaint to create an official government record (see Section 10)

**Step 2: Send Written Notice to Landlord**
- Must be in writing (email can work but certified mail is safest for proof of receipt)
- Must specify the material noncompliance (e.g., "failure to maintain elevator in compliance with the municipal code, in violation of RLTO Section 5-12-070")
- Must state the intended remedy (rent withholding amount, lease termination, etc.)
- Must give the landlord **at least 14 days** to cure the problem

**Step 3: Wait the Full 14-Day Cure Period**
- The 14-day period begins when the **landlord receives** the notice
- No remedies can be exercised until the 14 days have fully elapsed
- Example: Notice sent January 1 -> cure period ends January 14 -> rights activate January 15

**Step 4: Exercise Remedy (if landlord fails to cure)**
After 14 days with no cure, the tenant may:

- **Withhold rent** -- deduct an amount that "reasonably reflects the reduced value" of the premises
  - Conservative withholding (under 50% of monthly rent) carries lower legal risk
  - Must be reasonable and proportional to the impact on habitability
- **Terminate the lease** -- if the noncompliance renders premises "not reasonably fit and habitable," tenant may terminate with 30 additional days' notice
- **Repair and deduct** -- for issues costing $500 or less (or half monthly rent, whichever is greater), tenant may hire repairs and deduct from rent with paid receipts
- **Sue for damages** -- file suit to recover damages based on reduction in fair rental value, plus seek injunctive relief

### For Essential Services Failures (Accelerated Timeline)

- **24-hour notice** for rent withholding reflecting reduced value
- **72-hour notice** for lease termination
- May procure substitute services or substitute housing immediately

## 4. 24-Hour Notice Provision (Essential Services)

Under Section 5-12-110(f), when the landlord fails to provide essential services (heat, running water, hot water, electricity, gas, or plumbing), the tenant has an accelerated remedy:

- Tenant gives **written notice** requesting the landlord correct the failure
- If landlord fails to correct within **24 hours**, the tenant may **withhold from the monthly rent an amount that reasonably reflects the reduced value** of the premises
- Rent withholding cannot start until after the 24 hours expires
- Withholding applies only to days **past** the 24-hour period
- **No rent shall be withheld** if the failure is due to the inability of the utility provider to provide service

**Note:** While elevator failure is listed as "material noncompliance" in the RLTO, the 24-hour essential services provision specifically covers heat, running water, hot water, electricity, gas, and plumbing. Elevator failure falls under the **general 14-day notice provision** (subsection d) rather than the accelerated 24-hour provision, unless the elevator failure also creates an "immediate danger to the health and safety of the tenant" -- in which case the accelerated remedies under subsection (f) could potentially apply (e.g., for a disabled or elderly tenant in a high-rise with no elevator access).

## 5. 72-Hour Termination Provision

Under Section 5-12-110(f), for essential services failures or material noncompliance constituting an immediate danger to health and safety:

- If the material noncompliance or failure **persists for more than 72 hours** after the tenant has notified the landlord in writing
- The tenant may **terminate the rental agreement by written notice** to the landlord
- **Exception:** No termination shall be allowed if the failure is due to the inability of the utility provider to provide service

This is the fastest path to lease termination under the RLTO. Combined with the general 14-day termination provision in subsection (a), tenants have two different termination timelines depending on the severity of the issue.

## 6. Section 5-12-150: Prohibition on Retaliatory Conduct

**Source:** Chicago Municipal Code, Chapter 5-12, Section 5-12-150
**Official Code URL:** https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/chicago/latest/chicago_il/0-0-0-2639270

### Declaration of Public Policy

It is declared to be against public policy of the City of Chicago for a landlord to take retaliatory action against a tenant, except for violation of a rental agreement or violation of a law or ordinance.

### Protected Tenant Conduct

A tenant has the right to, without retaliation:
- **Complain** to governmental agencies, officials, police, media, community groups, tenant unions, or the landlord in good faith about tenancy conditions
- **Testify** in good faith about tenancy conditions
- **Exercise any right or remedy** provided by law
- **Request repairs** required by code or lease
- **Join or organize** a tenant union
- **File lawsuits** or administrative complaints

### Prohibited Landlord Actions

A landlord may NOT retaliate by:
- **Terminating** or threatening to terminate a tenancy
- **Increasing rent**
- **Decreasing services**
- **Bringing** or threatening to bring an eviction action
- **Refusing to renew** a lease agreement

### Rebuttable Presumption of Retaliation

If there is evidence of protected tenant conduct **within one year** prior to the alleged act of retaliation, that evidence creates a **rebuttable presumption** that the landlord's conduct was retaliatory. This means the burden shifts to the landlord to prove the action was NOT retaliatory.

### Tenant Remedies for Retaliation

If the landlord violates this section:
- The tenant has a **defense** in any retaliatory eviction action
- The tenant may **recover possession** or **terminate the rental agreement**
- In either case, the tenant may recover an amount equal to **not more than two months' rent OR twice the damages sustained**, whichever is greater
- Plus **reasonable attorney's fees**

### Practical Significance for Elevator Complaints

Tenants who complain about elevator failures, file 311 complaints, organize with neighbors, contact tenant unions, or exercise any RLTO remedy are **explicitly protected** from retaliation. If a landlord retaliates, the tenant can recover significant damages.

## 7. Section 5-12-180: Attorney's Fees

**Source:** Chicago Municipal Code, Chapter 5-12, Section 5-12-180
**Official Code URL:** https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/chicago/c7209359-81de-4059-a679f6a211f04dea/chicagobuilding_il/0-0-0-337234

### Statutory Language

> "Except in cases of forcible entry and detainer actions, the prevailing plaintiff in any action arising out of a landlord's or tenant's application of the rights or remedies made available in this ordinance shall be entitled to all court costs and reasonable attorney's fees; provided, however, that nothing herein shall be deemed or interpreted as precluding the awarding of attorney's fees in forcible entry and detainer actions in accordance with applicable law or as expressly provided in this ordinance."

### Key Points

1. **Fee-shifting provision:** The prevailing plaintiff recovers all court costs AND reasonable attorney's fees
2. **Applies to all RLTO claims** -- any action arising from rights or remedies in the ordinance
3. **Exception for eviction actions:** This fee-shifting does NOT apply to standard eviction (forcible entry and detainer) actions commenced by the landlord
4. **Encourages litigation:** By shifting fees, the RLTO makes it financially viable for tenants to bring claims even for moderate damages, since the landlord will pay the tenant's attorney fees if the tenant prevails
5. **"Prevailing plaintiff" standard** -- the party who initiates the action (plaintiff) must win to recover fees; this primarily benefits tenants suing landlords for RLTO violations

### Practical Significance

This provision is a powerful incentive for tenants and their attorneys. A tenant who sues over elevator failure and wins can recover:
- Damages reflecting reduced rental value
- Injunctive relief (court order requiring repairs)
- All court costs
- All reasonable attorney's fees

This makes it easier for tenants to find attorneys willing to take RLTO cases on contingency or reduced fee arrangements.

## 8. Material Noncompliance: Complete Enumerated List

**Source:** Section 5-12-110, definition of material noncompliance with Section 5-12-070

The following failures constitute material noncompliance. This list is **non-exhaustive** ("includes, but is not limited to"):

1. Failure to maintain the **structural integrity** of the building or structure or parts thereof
2. Failure to maintain **floors** in compliance with the safe load-bearing requirements of the municipal code
3. Failure to comply with applicable requirements for the number, width, construction, location, or accessibility of **exits**
4. Failure to maintain **exit, stairway, fire escape, or directional signs** where required by the municipal code
5. Failure to provide **smoke detectors, sprinkler systems, standpipe systems, fire alarm systems, automatic fire detectors, or fire extinguishers** where required by the municipal code
6. **Failure to maintain elevators in compliance with applicable provisions of the municipal code** [KEY PROVISION FOR ELEVATOR CASES]
7. Failure to provide or maintain in good working order a **flush water closet, lavatory basin, bathtub or shower, or kitchen sink**
8. Failure to maintain **plumbing facilities, piping, fixtures, appurtenances, and appliances**
9. Failure to provide **adequate heat** (68 degrees F daytime / 66 degrees F nighttime, September through June)
10. Failure to provide **hot and cold running water** as required by the municipal code
11. Failure to provide **electricity or gas service**
12. Failure to maintain **gas-fired appliances** in safe working condition
13. Failure to maintain the **dwelling unit and common areas in a fit and habitable condition**
14. Failure to maintain **floors, interior walls, or ceilings** in sound condition
15. Failure to maintain the **basement or cellar** in a safe and sanitary condition
16. Failure to provide or maintain **electrical systems, circuits, receptacles, and devices**
17. Failure to maintain **windows, exterior doors, or basement hatchways** in sound condition
18. Failure to provide **screens** (during applicable season)
19. Failure to maintain **stairways and porches** in safe condition
20. Failure to provide **adequate hall or stairway lighting** as required by the municipal code
21. Failure to prevent the **accumulation of stagnant water**
22. Failure to **exterminate insects, rodents, or other pests**
23. Failure to supply or maintain facilities for **refuse disposal**
24. Failure to provide **adequate light or ventilation** as required by the municipal code
25. Failure to maintain **chimneys** in safe working condition

**Source for complete list:** Chirica Law RLTO Notice Generator (https://tools.chirica.law/habitability/) which draws from the statutory text of Section 5-12-110.

## 9. Chicago Municipal Code: Elevator Maintenance & Inspection

### Governing Code Sections

- **Chapter 14X-10:** Elevators and Other Conveyance Devices
  - URL: https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/chicago/latest/chicago_il/0-0-0-2676349
- **Section 13-20-100:** Annual inspection requirements for conveying devices
- **Section 18-30-017:** AIC compliance requirements
- **Section 18-30-430:** Penalties for AIC violations

### Annual Inspection Certification (AIC) Program

The City of Chicago requires building owners/property managers to participate in the Annual Inspection Certification program:

- **Requirement:** Building owners must **hire state-licensed, third-party inspection companies** to inspect elevators and other conveying devices **annually**
- **Reporting:** Results must be reported to the Department of Buildings via the online AIC portal (https://ipi.cityofchicago.org/AIC)
- **Standard:** Inspections follow ASME A17.1 national standards for "periodic inspections"
- **Quarterly Due Dates:** The upgraded system uses quarterly due dates for participants
- **Scope:** Covers passenger elevators, freight elevators, escalators, dumbwaiters, moving walks, material handling devices, personnel hoists, and vertical platforms
- **Contact:** aic@cityofchicago.org

### Building Owner Responsibilities

- Negotiate, contract, and pay for the services of an Authorized Technician for annual inspections
- Correct issues identified during inspections **within 60 days** of notification (or prior to expiration of a Conditional Certificate)
- Submit online forms and payment by the due date
- Maintain elevators in compliance with all applicable code provisions

### Penalties for Non-Compliance

- Violation of AIC requirements under Section 18-30-017 subjects the owner to a fine of **not less than $200 nor more than $500 for each offense**
- **Each day** that a violation continues constitutes a **separate and distinct offense** with a separate fine
- If an owner fails to submit the AIC form and payment by the due date, the address is forwarded to **Administrative Hearings** for adjudication
- Fines of **$200 to $500 per day per device** may be assessed at Administrative Hearings
- These fines are in addition to any late filing fee or other penalty provided by law

### Chicago Department of Buildings -- Elevator Bureau

- **Phone:** 312-743-3594
- **In-Person Hours:** Wednesdays, 9 AM - 3 PM, Room 900 City Hall (121 North LaSalle Street)
- **Responsibilities:** Permit inspections, accident investigations, periodic inspections, AIC program audits, 311 service request responses
- **Approximate inventory:** ~22,000 elevators and moving conveyances in Chicago

### Elevator Advisory Committee

An Elevator Advisory Committee of industry experts assists the DOB with code updates and evaluates new conveyance technology.

## 10. Filing a 311 Complaint for Elevator Issues

### How to File

There are three ways to report elevator issues to the City of Chicago:

1. **Call 311** (non-emergency city services line)
2. **Online:** Visit https://311.chicago.gov and select "Home and Buildings" > "Safety" > look for "Elevator Safety Concern, Occupied Building"
3. **CHI311 App** on your phone

### What to Report

Report the elevator as a building code violation. The Chicago building code covers elevator service as one of the regulated conditions. Specify:
- Building address
- Which elevator(s) are affected
- How long the elevator has been out of service
- Any safety hazards

### Anonymity

- Reports **can be made anonymously**
- However, to have a building inspector contact you directly, provide your name and daytime phone number

### What Happens After Filing

1. You receive a **tracking number** (request one if not automatically provided)
2. The Department of Buildings **sends an inspector** to verify the complaint
3. If violations are found, the landlord receives a **citation with 15 days to address** the problem
4. Failure to comply may result in **court action**
5. Contact your **alderman** with your tracking number for additional support and political pressure

### Why Filing 311 Matters

- Creates an **official government record** of the problem
- Triggers a **government inspection**
- The record supports any future legal claims under the RLTO
- Filing a 311 complaint is **protected tenant activity** under Section 5-12-150 (retaliation protection)
- Your landlord **cannot evict you or raise your rent** because you filed a complaint

## Summary: Elevator-Specific Legal Framework

### The Legal Chain for Elevator Failures

1. **Landlord Obligation (5-12-070):** Landlord must maintain premises in compliance with all municipal code provisions, including elevator code (Chapter 14X-10).

2. **Material Noncompliance (5-12-110):** Failure to maintain elevators in compliance with the municipal code is explicitly listed as material noncompliance.

3. **Tenant Remedies (5-12-110):**
   - **14-day written notice** to landlord specifying elevator noncompliance
   - After 14 days without cure: **rent withholding** (amount reflecting reduced value), **lease termination** (if premises not reasonably fit and habitable), **repair and deduct** (for issues under $500), or **lawsuit for damages and injunctive relief**
   - For essential services failures / immediate danger: **24-hour notice** for rent withholding, **72-hour notice** for lease termination

4. **Retaliation Protection (5-12-150):** Tenant is protected from retaliation for exercising any of these rights. Landlord who retaliates owes up to 2 months' rent or 2x damages.

5. **Attorney's Fees (5-12-180):** Prevailing plaintiff recovers all court costs and reasonable attorney's fees.

6. **City Enforcement:** 311 complaint triggers DOB inspection; violations result in citations with 15-day cure period; non-compliance may lead to court action. AIC violations carry $200-$500/day/device fines.

### RLTO Applicability Note

The RLTO applies to most residential rental properties in Chicago. It does **NOT** apply to:
- Owner-occupied buildings with 6 or fewer units
- Hotels, motels, and rooming houses
- Dormitories, shelters, and employee quarters
- Non-residential rentals
- Owner-occupied cooperatives

For tenants not covered by the RLTO, Illinois state landlord-tenant law still provides protections.

## Key Legal Takeaways for Chicago Tenants with Elevator Outages

1. **Elevators are part of the "premises"** under the RLTO -- loss of elevator service = loss of use of a portion of the premises.
2. **14-day written notice required** before exercising remedies (rent withholding, repair-and-deduct, or lease termination).
3. **Rent withholding** is capped at $500 or half the monthly rent, whichever is greater.
4. **Lease termination** is available if the problem persists for 72+ hours after notice (for conditions affecting health/safety).
5. **311 complaints** create a paper trail and trigger city inspections; landlords get 15 days from citation to fix the problem.
6. **Free legal help** is available through Law Center for Better Housing (lcbh.org) and Uptown People's Law Center (773-769-1411).
